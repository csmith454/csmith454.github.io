Not even two hours ago I just about the craziest headline from AP that I could have imagined.  I had to look up the keywords on Google just to make sure my phone had not been hacked.  

**Trump says US to get diesel from Russia, relaxing pressure on Moscow to ease prices before midterms**

Elections make leaders do crazy things...  But my first thoughts were not about President Trump and his upcoming midterms (may there be retribution), they were with the policy makers and academics who work tirelessly on keeping up effective sanctions.  The United States will now buy nearly two million tons of diesel from Russia after four years of the largest sanctions regime the world has seen.  Policy makers made massive strides over the last decade to pinpoint what type of economic activity could be punished to make target states feel the burn.  Trump has provided a potent anti-burn balm.  Academics monitored at the edge of their seat for every new turn addition to the global economic sanctions against Russia.  Trump has provided them with a fantastic shock for research purposes (I can only imagine how incredible a differences-in-differences methodology could be employed with the right data).  Nevermind the hypocrisy of the US buying diesel while secondary sanctions ensure legal action against those supporting the Russian war effort through energy purchases. 

While this revelation stunts the work of policy makers and makes academics queasy and excited at the same time, the realities of life on the ground in Ukraine could not be more impacted.  All for the chance to lower diesel prices domestically to gain an advantage during this November's midterms.
